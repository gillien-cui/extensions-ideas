# Reverse Lunge

**Targets:** quads, glutes, balance · **Equipment:** 2 dumbbells · **Used in:** Day C · **Reps:** 2 × 12–15 per leg

**Demo (photos + video):** [ACE — Reverse Lunge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/319/reverse-lunge/) · [NASM — Squat Alternatives (reverse lunge)](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/squat-alternatives)

Stepping **back** is easier on the knees than stepping forward.

## Setup
- Stand tall with a dumbbell in each hand at your sides, feet hip-width apart.

## How to do it
1. Step one foot back about 2 feet (60 cm) and land on the ball of that foot.
2. Lower straight down until your back knee almost touches the floor. Both knees are at about 90°.
3. Push through the **front heel** to return to standing.
4. Do all reps on one leg, then switch (or alternate legs).

## Cues
- Torso upright, or a slight forward lean for more glute work.
- Front knee tracks over the middle of your foot.
- Think "elevator down, elevator up", not forward and back.

## Common mistakes
- Step too short → front heel lifts, knee shoots forward.
- Pushing off the back foot → most of the work should come from the front leg.
- Wobbling → use lighter dumbbells or hold one dumbbell and keep a hand on a rack.

## Easier / harder
- **Easier:** bodyweight, or hold onto something for balance.
- **Harder:** heavier dumbbells, a 3-second lowering, or the front foot raised on a small plate.
