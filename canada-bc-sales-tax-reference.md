# Canada — British Columbia: Sales Tax on Purchased Items

**Short answer: 12% total on most retail purchases in B.C. — 5% federal GST + 7% provincial PST.**

B.C. is *not* an HST province. GST and PST are two separate taxes, each calculated on the
selling price (they are **not** compounded — PST is not charged on the GST). A $100 item
costs $112.00: $5.00 GST + $7.00 PST.

*Last verified: September 2026. Rates change — confirm against the primary sources linked at
the bottom before relying on this for pricing, invoicing, or filing.*

---

## 1. Headline rates

| Tax | Rate | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| GST (federal) | 5% | Most goods and services, including many things PST exempts (e.g. restaurant meals) |
| PST (provincial) | 7% (general) | Most tangible goods, software, telecom, and a growing list of services |
| **Combined** | **12%** | The default assumption for a taxable retail item in B.C. |

## 2. PST rates that are *not* 7%

| Item | PST rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Liquor | 10% | Applies at retail and in restaurants/bars |
| Short-term accommodation | 8% | Plus up to 3% MRDT (municipal & regional district tax) in participating areas |
| Passenger vehicle — bought from a GST registrant (dealer) | 7% / 8% / 9% / 10% / 15% / 20% | Tiered by price: 7% under $55,000, stepping up at $55,000, $56,000 and $57,000, then 15% at $125,000 and 20% at $150,000+ |
| Passenger vehicle — private sale or taxable gift | 12% / 15% / 20% | 12% under $125,000; 15% for $125,000–$149,999.99; 20% at $150,000+. ICBC charges tax on the purchase price *or* Canadian Black Book wholesale value, whichever is higher |
| Soda / sweetened carbonated beverages | 7% | Taxable since April 1, 2021 — an exception to the general food exemption |

Exact vehicle bands are set out in Bulletin PST 308 (Vehicles); verify there before quoting a
number on a high-value car, since the step thresholds are narrow.

## 3. Commonly exempt from PST (but usually still 5% GST)

- Basic groceries and **restaurant meals / prepared food** (PST-exempt; GST still applies; liquor is 10% PST)
- Children's clothing and footwear (age 14 and under)
- Prescription drugs, vitamins and dietary supplements
- Non-motorized adult-sized bicycles
- Books, newspapers and magazines
- Fuel taxed under the *Motor Fuel Tax Act*
- Most services historically — though this is the category that is shrinking fastest (see §5)

Basic groceries are zero-rated for GST as well, so a bag of flour carries no tax at all;
a restaurant meal carries 5% only.

## 4. Selling *into* B.C. — when you must collect

If you sell taxable goods, **software**, or telecommunication services to B.C. customers,
you must register for and collect PST once gross revenue from those B.C. sales exceeds
**CAD $10,000 in any rolling 12-month period**. This applies to sellers located in other
provinces and outside Canada. Software and telecom revenue share a single $10,000 threshold —
they are not counted separately.

**SaaS is taxable software in B.C.** Cloud-based offerings — SaaS, IaaS, APIs and related
online services — are treated as taxable software at 7%, regardless of how the software is
accessed or where it is hosted. This matters for any product idea in this workspace that
would bill B.C. customers directly.

The federal side is separate: GST/HST registration is generally required once worldwide
taxable revenue exceeds $30,000 in a rolling four-quarter window (voluntary registration is
available below that, and is usually worth it if you have input tax credits to claim).

## 5. What changed for 2026 — PST expansion to professional services

B.C.'s Budget 2026 (announced February 17, 2026) leaves the 7% rate alone but widens the
base. **Effective October 1, 2026**, PST at 7% applies to:

| Newly taxable service | Taxable base |
|---|---|
| Accounting, bookkeeping and assurance services | 100% of the fee |
| Architectural services | **30%** of the fee (effective 2.1%) |
| Engineering and geoscience services | **30%** of the fee (effective 2.1%) |
| Non-residential real estate services — trading commissions, rental property management, strata management | 100% of the fee |
| Security services, including private investigation | 100% of the fee |

The 30% rule means $100,000 of engineering work carries $2,100 of PST (7% × $30,000), not $7,000.

Also effective October 1, 2026, exemptions are removed for:

- Certain clothing-related materials, and services related to clothing and footwear
- Basic cable TV, toll-free telephone, and residential landline services

Providers of any newly taxable service who are not already registered must register before
that date. Details are in **Notice 2026-001**.

## 6. Practical notes

- Quote consumer prices as pre-tax unless you have a reason not to — B.C. retail convention is tax added at the till.
- For a B.C.-based one-person software business: your own purchases of taxable goods and software carry 7% PST as a real, unrecoverable cost. Unlike GST (recoverable via input tax credits), **PST paid on business inputs is generally not refundable** — there is no PST equivalent of an ITC, only narrow exemptions for goods bought for resale or for specific production uses.
- The professional-services expansion means your accountant's and bookkeeper's invoices get 7% more expensive from October 1, 2026, with no offset.
